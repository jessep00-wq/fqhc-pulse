## Post 1 — "What HRSA reviewers actually open first"

After sitting in on enough OSVs, I noticed something:

Reviewers don't read your binder front to back.

They open three things, in this order:
1. The QI/QA committee minutes from the **last 3 months**.
2. One specific UDS measure they pre-flagged (usually whichever one your last report missed target on).
3. The PDSA cycle that's supposed to be moving that measure.

If those three documents don't tell a coherent story — measure → cycle → minute → next cycle — the rest of the binder doesn't save you.

What gets most FQHCs is not missing PDSA work. It's that the work lives in five different places: SharePoint, Excel, the EMR, a clinical director's inbox, and one person's head.

The fix isn't more documentation. It's one place where the PDSA cycle, the UDS measure it's tied to, and the board minute that approved it all live together.

That's the actual job. Everything else is formatting.

## Post 2 — "The PDSA cycle most QI teams skip"

There are four phases in PDSA. Plan, Do, Study, Act.

In practice, most FQHC QI teams I've worked with do Plan and Do beautifully. They get stuck at Study, and they almost never document Act.

Here's why that matters for HRSA OSV:

A PDSA cycle that ends at "Do" is just an intervention. A cycle that ends at "Act" is **evidence of a learning organization** — which is literally the standard HRSA reviewers are measuring you against.

The fix is small: when you close a cycle, write two sentences.
- "We tried X. Measure moved from Y to Z."
- "Next cycle: we are changing [one thing] because [one reason]."

That's it. Two sentences turn a documentation artifact into evidence of a QI culture.

If your team is doing the clinical work but the binder doesn't tell that story, the binder is the problem. Not the work.

— Jessica

## Post 3 — "The dashboard isn't the deliverable"

Talked to a QI Director last week who said her team built a beautiful Tableau dashboard for UDS measures.

It updates nightly. It's color-coded. The clinical staff loves it.

Her HRSA reviewer didn't open it once.

Reviewers want a binder. PDF. Sequential. Cross-referenced.

That's not a critique of the dashboard — dashboards are how you **run** the work. But the binder is how you **prove** the work.

If your team is investing in one without the other, you're going to feel that gap during OSV. The good news: the binder isn't a separate project. It's a different view of the same data. You just need a system that can produce both.

Curious — for QI Directors here, which one is your team weaker on right now: running the work, or proving it?

— Jessica
