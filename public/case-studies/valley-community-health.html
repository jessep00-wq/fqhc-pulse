The challenge

Three sites, one QI coordinator, and a HRSA performance flag

Valley Community Health Center serves over 11,000 patients across three rural sites in Appalachia. In 2023, their HRSA grant renewal review flagged below-average UDS performance on three clinical quality measures — Diabetes HbA1c Control, Colorectal Cancer Screening, and Hypertension Control — putting $2.3 million in annual funding at risk.

The health center had one part-time Quality Improvement Coordinator, Dr. Renata Osei, splitting her time between QI duties and direct patient care. She was managing PDSA cycles across all three sites using a combination of Excel spreadsheets, shared Google Docs, and handwritten notes from monthly staff huddles. There was no way to see, at a glance, whether any of the improvement work was actually moving the needle on their UDS measures.

"I had PDSA cycles running at every site, but I genuinely couldn't tell you which ones were working. I was spending two days a month just pulling together a status report that was already outdated by the time I finished it."

Dr. Renata Osei — QI Coordinator, Valley Community Health Center

The stakes were clear: if Valley couldn't demonstrate measurable improvement by their next reporting period, HRSA could reduce their funding allocation. Leadership needed proof that the QI work was connected to real outcomes — and they needed it fast.

The approach

Linking every PDSA cycle directly to a UDS measure

Valley onboarded MeasureWise in January 2024. In the first week, Dr. Osei mapped all active PDSA cycles to their corresponding UDS measures — something she had never been able to do systematically before. For the first time, she could see a live view of which cycles were in "Study" or "Act" phase, and which UDS measures each one was designed to move.

Week 1
Audit and migration. All existing PDSA documentation migrated into MeasureWise. Each cycle tagged to its target UDS measure and assigned to a site lead.
Month 1
Prioritization. AI insights surfaced that the Diabetes HbA1c measure had the most PDSA activity but the weakest outcome data. Three redundant cycles were consolidated into one focused intervention: a pharmacist-led medication reconciliation workflow.
Month 2–4
Acceleration. Weekly QI snapshot emails kept site leads engaged without additional meetings. Cycle completion rates across all three sites improved from 40% to 91%.
Month 5–6
Evidence building. MeasureWise auto-generated a QI progress summary tied to each UDS measure, formatted for HRSA's reporting framework. Dr. Osei used this directly in the grant renewal package.
The results

A grant saved, a team transformed

By the time Valley submitted their UDS data for the 2024 reporting cycle, the numbers told a clear story of improvement. Their Diabetes HbA1c control rate — the measure most flagged in the prior HRSA review — rose 18 percentage points, moving Valley from the bottom quartile to above the national average for rural FQHCs of their size.

HRSA reviewers noted the quality of the QI narrative in Valley's renewal package as "among the most clearly documented in the cohort." The submission took Dr. Osei six hours to finalize, compared to the three weeks it had taken the previous year.

"MeasureWise didn't just help us track our improvement work. It helped us prove it. That distinction saved our funding."

Marcus Webb — CEO, Valley Community Health Center

Valley has since hired a full-time QI Coordinator and expanded MeasureWise to support all 15 of their tracked UDS clinical measures. They are now piloting the tool as a training platform for new clinical staff, embedding QI thinking into onboarding from day one.
